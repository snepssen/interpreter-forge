# Interpreter for Android

The Interpreter as a native Android app: Kotlin, Jetpack Compose, the same
engine design as the iPhone app, and the same voice, held to the same fixtures.

    . scripts/env.sh                         # Java 21 and the Android SDK
    ./gradlew :voice:connectedDebugAndroidTest   # on a phone or the emulator (interpreter-pixel)
    scripts/install-app.sh                    # the app; --models TRANSLATE_GEMMA copies local models in

## Built in phases

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **voice** | espeak-ng built here from the pinned revision 724808c with voice-core's options; Polyphon in Kotlin; the Piper model on ONNX Runtime. Held to voice-core's fixtures: Polyphon id for id against the Python, English phonemes as Voice Forge's build gave them, Gateway Forge's 481 calls. Done: all pass on the emulator, and the epoch-200 voice speaks all eight languages at about 15x real time. |
| 2 | **hearing** | Silero VAD v5 on ONNX Runtime (the iPhone app's class, line for line) and Parakeet Ultra (moondream's post-training of v3, as the Apple apps use, int8 in sherpa-onnx's layout, checksum-verified) through sherpa-onnx, tokens joined into timed words as on the iPhone. Done: on the emulator snepssen's eight recordings are heard at 79-98% of words (v3: 71-97), 20-40x real time (`scripts/fetch-hearing.sh`, `scripts/test-hearing.sh`). |
| 3 | **translating** | TranslateGemma 4B and EuroLLM 1.7B (Q4_K_M GGUF, pinned, SHA-256-checked) on llama.cpp v0.7.0, built here with llama.cpp's own Android settings: seven CPU variants from armv8.0 to armv9.2, the best chosen at run time, so a budget phone runs the slower path instead of crashing. The context is reused across calls like the iPhone app's PromptCache. Prompts are rendered exactly as the models' own chat templates do (held to prompts.json from the Python tokenizers, quirks included) and tokenized exactly as their own tokenizers do (EuroLLM needs its GGUF's space prefix overridden). Done: on the emulator TranslateGemma translates in 1.3-2.2 s a line, EuroLLM in 0.4 s (`scripts/fetch-translating.sh`, `scripts/test-translating.sh`). |
| 4 | **the interpreter** | `core`, plain Kotlin: the segmenter, clock times, language evidence, echoes, catch-up and the quality checks (the commentary guard among them), the iPhone app's InterpreterCore ported and held to the same 53 cases its tccheck holds it to (`./gradlew :core:test`, seconds, no device). `engine`: the Linguist (the gate, the source language, what is said of an output), Android's own language detector where the iPhone asks Apple's (75 of 76 short lines of known language, like Apple's; agrees with it on 97% of 2,341 heard lines), and the live loop from microphone to headphones. Hearing and translating take turns at the CPU instead of sharing it: on the emulator's four cores that took the median lag on snepssen's recordings from 14.5 s to 5.8 s (90th percentile 8.0 s), TranslateGemma translating in 1.4-2.5 s a line (`scripts/test-engine.sh`). Done. |
| 5 | **the app** | Jetpack Compose, Material 3 in the phone's own colours: the iPhone app's screens. The language menu (any language, or only these, into one of the voice's eight; untrained languages behind a setting, off by default), the transcript with the newest translation large, the Live button with the microphone level around it, captions only, volume. On first launch the translator is chosen and downloaded (resumed if the connection drops, kept only if its SHA-256 matches), with speech recognition: TranslateGemma on phones with 6 GB, EuroLLM on 4 GB ones, and an honest "can't run here" below that. Summarise to keep up stays off: neither translator takes instructions. Interpreting is foreground-only, as on the iPhone. Tried on the emulator from a fresh install: the translator and speech recognition downloaded, resumed after the app was killed, checked and loaded; snepssen's recordings interpreted on screen and aloud (`scripts/install-app.sh`, and `scripts/demo-app.sh pl` to play a recording in as the microphone in a debug build). Phones and tablets, as the iPhone and iPad app: landscape puts the controls in a panel beside the conversation (on a phone on its side too, scrolling when short), tablets get larger type and a larger button, a text size from 75% to 400% (Settings, the panel, Ctrl and minus or plus, or TalkBack's adjust), and a dark screen while live. Tried on the emulator as a phone and as a 10-inch tablet (`adb shell wm size 1600x2560; adb shell wm density 320`), both ways up, through a rotation mid-session. The emulator needs `-memory 8192` for TranslateGemma beside Google's apps. Still to try on a real phone. |

The `voice` module mirrors voice-core and is meant to move there once the app
is proven, so every Android app that speaks with snepssen gets it from one
place, as the Apple ones do. Its espeak-ng data and parity fixtures are
copied from a voice-core checkout beside interpreter-forge at build time, never
kept twice; the speaking test takes the newest polyglot voice the way the
Apple builds do (`../apple/scripts/polyglot-voice.sh`).
